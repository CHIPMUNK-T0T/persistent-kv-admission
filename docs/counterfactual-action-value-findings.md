# One-step counterfactual retention action values: findings

This is the descriptive result of the [pre-registered experiment](counterfactual-action-value-plan.md), using the frozen implementation at commit `4317858`. The question is whether exact future-reuse labels choose the same L2 eviction action as realized avoided-prefill-token value. For each already-drawn candidate set, regret is the largest realized continuation value `Q` among its candidates minus `Q` for a selector's choice. The primary reward window is `(t, t + 600 s]`; the secondary window ends at the trace end. Each branch forces **one** victim, then resumes its frozen source policy. These are conditional finite-trace values, not estimates of a new deployed policy.

## Completion and validation

The run selected eight decisions by pre-frozen identity hash from each of 40 published test populations: two traces, two cache cells, two source policies, and five lineage seeds. It retained all 320 decisions, 8,143 candidate/stream continuations, and both reward windows. Candidate sets contained 17 actions in 311 decisions and 16 in nine. All 40 uninterrupted parents matched their published utility rows. All 320 captured-stream actual-action branches matched their parent request-reward digest, both window rewards, terminal state, and replay counters. The runner checked the saved candidate identities, score tuples, labels, and original victim before each fork. There were no failed lineages. The selection manifest, source/model/population/trace hashes, protected documentation diffs, and all retained branch file hashes passed the recorded checks. An independent review also reproduced the 4,800 regret rows' arithmetic, the 640 primary exact-label tie envelopes, and the action-level L1/L2 difference invariant from the published tables. The additional plots below read only the frozen CSVs; their script, input, and output hashes are in [plot_review_provenance.json](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/plot_review_provenance.json).

The detailed evidence is in [selected_decisions.csv](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/selected_decisions.csv), [branch_actions.csv](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/branch_actions.csv), [decision_regrets.csv](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/decision_regrets.csv), [seed_regrets.csv](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/seed_regrets.csv), [decision_type_regrets.csv](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/decision_type_regrets.csv), [stratified_regrets.csv](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/stratified_regrets.csv), and [sensitivity.csv](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/sensitivity.csv). The [run config](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/run_config.json) identifies the source hashes and audit run. Per-seed tables report the predeclared mean, median, 90th percentile, maximum, zero-regret rate, and fraction with regret at least 512 tokens; decision rows preserve every value and tie count. The 512-token cutoff is a reporting scale only.

## Primary 600-second result

Each row below has 40 decisions, eight from each of five seeds. Values are mean regret in avoided-prefill tokens. “Exact” is the predeclared next-use selector with its causal last-group/draw-order tie-break; `Exact − learned` is paired within each decision. “Best in tie” selects retrospectively by `Q` only among candidates tied for the exact label's minimum. It is a hindsight diagnostic bound, **not** an implementable selector. “Tie excludes best” counts decisions where even that best tied candidate has positive regret.

The exact selector has lower mean regret than learned in five strata and higher in three. These comparisons pair selectors on the **same** sampled states; the pi0 and pi3 populations are different states and are not a causal comparison between source policies. Across strata, learned median regrets range from 5,120 to 55,189 tokens, 90th percentiles from 26,675 to 107,681, and maxima from 35,971 to 141,265. Exact median regrets range from 10,240 to 46,325, 90th percentiles from 29,064 to 99,074, and maxima from 48,128 to 136,145. Learned has zero regret in 5–25% of decisions per stratum, exact in 0–12.5%; at least 512-token regret occurs in 72.5–95% and 87.5–100%, respectively. Full precision and all five-seed distributions are in the linked CSVs. There are **no constant-`Q`** candidate sets: the per-stratum mean `Q` range is 31,677–140,968 tokens, with a largest individual range of 236,014. A zero-regret selector can still coexist with a nonconstant candidate set.

The exact next-use and exact-count selectors chose the same victim at all 320 decisions. The minimum count was zero at every decision, and their causal tie-break then agreed; they are not two independent confirmations. The exact selector agreed with the source learned victim at only 14/320 decisions. The minimum exact-label tie had a mean of 9.8875 candidates, and 318/320 decisions had more than one such candidate. In 222/320 decisions the tie contained a `Q`-maximizing action; in 98/320 it excluded every maximizer (72/160 in the small cell, 26/160 in the large cell). The fixed tie-break selected a worse action than the hindsight best tied candidate in 280/320 decisions. Over these equally sampled 320 states, mean exact regret was 32,223.6 tokens and mean hindsight best-in-tie regret was 3,660.8; the large gap diagnoses missing action information **within** the exact-label tie. The 98 positive best-in-tie regrets also show a label-order failure on those evaluated states. Neither number measures trace-wide recoverable loss.

Arrival rejection was the original action in 139/160 small-cell decisions and 0/160 large-cell decisions. [decision_type_regrets.csv](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/decision_type_regrets.csv) retains those descriptive denominators; the table above does not erase this composition difference. Mean within-decision Spearman correlation of retention score with `-Q(evict)` was 0.031 for learned and 0.116 for exact next-use in the primary captured stream. It was undefined for three exact-label decisions with constant candidate labels; there were no constant-`Q` decisions. These rank summaries are secondary to paired regret.

## Secondary horizon and continuation randomness

The trace-end horizon varies by selected timestamp, approximately 600–1,413 seconds. Across the equally sampled 320 states, the fixed exact selector's secondary mean regret was 44,933.1 tokens, while the hindsight best-in-tie mean was 4,991.9; the best tie still had positive regret at 105/320 decisions. The secondary comparison can change direction within a stratum: for conversation 0.02 × 4 pi0, exact versus learned mean regret is 51,372 versus 56,176 at 600 seconds, but 81,906 versus 74,243 through trace end. These horizons were fixed before results and should not be interpreted as interchangeable.

On the first two hash-ranked decisions per lineage (80 states), every action was rerun with two derived post-draw RNG seeds. Each stream has its own actual-action control, and [sensitivity.csv](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/sensitivity.csv) averages three separately paired stream regrets. For those same 80 states in the primary window, captured-stream mean regret was 34,248 for exact and 34,210 for learned; the three-stream means were 34,419 and 35,107, giving an exact-minus-learned paired mean of −688 tokens. The sign of that paired difference, counting zero separately, differed among the three streams for 51/80 individual decisions. The same-80 stratum mean signs agreed between captured-only and three-stream summaries, although individual action values were sensitive to the continuation stream. Three streams do not provide a well-estimated expectation, and the 80-state sensitivity subset should not be compared as if it were the full 320-state sample.

## Reading the figures and limits

[Primary selector figure](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/primary_600s_regret_reviewed.png) and [secondary selector figure](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/secondary_trace_end_regret_reviewed.png) show mean regret by trace, cache cell, source policy, and selector. A bar compares selectors on states induced by its own source policy; pi0 and pi3 bars describe different populations. [Primary tie figure](../results/paper/counterfactual_action_001/primary_600s_label_tie_reviewed.png) juxtaposes learned, fixed exact-label, and hindsight best-in-tie means; its green bar is a diagnostic lower envelope, not achievable policy performance. None of the figures establishes workload-general effects or total recoverable trace utility. The original pre-registered figure files and their hashes remain unchanged in the paper directory.

These results show sizable realized single-decision regret for both learned and fixed exact-label choices on the sampled states, with a prominent within-label-tie gap and a smaller but nonzero label-order gap. `Q` includes the entire downstream cache trajectory after the one forced victim and may include different later sampled candidate populations and RNG consumption. It cannot be decomposed here into independent “one block,” trajectory, and randomness contributions. The maximum `Q` is chosen in hindsight within each stream, so regret is a diagnostic, not an expected improvement available to a deployable selector. The source traces and held-out windows also informed earlier research decisions; five seeds share future requests and do not support workload-general significance. The experiment does not identify whether a learned-model gap comes from features or fitting, establish semantic necessity, or justify extrapolating sampled regrets to trace-wide loss.
